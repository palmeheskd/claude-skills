# Brainstorm

Generates a range of ideas across different angles — not just the obvious ones.

## The job

A useful brainstorm isn't a list of 10 variations on the same idea. It covers
different angles: safe options alongside unexpected ones, quick wins alongside
bigger bets, conventional approaches alongside left-field ones. The user should
finish reading it with at least one idea they wouldn't have thought of themselves.

## Before generating ideas

Understand the actual problem first. Ask yourself:

- What is the user trying to achieve?
- What constraints are real (time, budget, audience, format)?
- What have they already tried or ruled out? If the user hasn't said, ask briefly — one question, not a form.

Don't start listing ideas until you understand what a good idea looks like here.

## How to structure the output

Group ideas by angle, not by quality. Typical angles:

- **Quick / low-effort** — things they could do today
- **Conventional** — established approaches that work reliably
- **Unexpected** — ideas that flip the usual assumption
- **High-effort / high-reward** — bigger bets worth considering if there's appetite

Not every category applies to every prompt. Use the ones that are genuinely
relevant — don't force the structure if it doesn't fit.

For each idea, give:
1. A short name or headline (so ideas are scannable)
2. One or two sentences on what it involves and why it's worth considering
3. Any honest tradeoff or catch, if there is one

## Push past the obvious

The first few ideas that come to mind are usually the ones the user already
thought of. After listing the conventional options, ask: what would someone
from a completely different field do here? What's the opposite of the obvious
approach? What would work if the usual constraints didn't apply?

Include at least one idea the user might initially dismiss — explain why it's
worth a second look.

## Length

Aim for 6–10 ideas across 2–4 groups. More than that and it becomes noise.
Fewer than 5 and it's not really a brainstorm.

## After the list

End with one short line: which idea you'd start with and why — your actual
recommendation, not a hedge. The user can ignore it, but a point of view is
more useful than "it depends on your goals."
